1 · Arrive

Fairness is not only sameness

In Chapter 5, you learned that a rule can look equal on paper while working very differently in real life. This workbook helps you test whether a rule creates real capability, not merely formal permission.

2 · Read the Case

Equal Access on Paper

Case: A college moves all course materials online. The policy has good intentions: online materials can reduce costs, make updates easier, and let students access work from many places.

The college announces, “All students now have equal access to course materials online.” But some students have fast internet, updated laptops, quiet rooms, flexible schedules, and reliable transportation. Other students rely on phones, shared devices, unstable internet, crowded living spaces, work schedules, caregiving responsibilities, or public transportation.

On paper, everyone has access. In reality, access is easier for some students than for others.

3 · Rule and Unknown Position

Apply the veil of ignorance

Veil of ignorance: a fairness test that asks whether you would accept a rule if you did not know what position you would occupy under it.
4 · Benefits and Burdens

Follow the burden without ignoring the benefits

A rule can have real benefits and still distribute burdens unfairly. Chapter 5 asks you to see both.

5 · Permission vs Capability

Distinguish equal wording from real access

Formal permission: what the rule allows on paper.
Real capability: the actual conditions needed to do or be something important.
6 · Care and Moral Commons

Notice dependency and shared conditions

Moral commons: shared conditions such as trust, safety, voice, fairness, participation, and repair.
7 · Responsibility and Limits

Preserve responsibility without pretending the map is complete

Chapter 5 does not say standards should disappear. It asks whether standards are connected to real routes for meeting them.

8 · Justice Repair

Make the rule route into real participation

Justice repair changes a rule, route, support, or condition so participation becomes more real and fair.
